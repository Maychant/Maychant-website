# Maychant homepage: content and product review

Prepared 4 November 2026. This review applies the completed succession strategy research to the first usable homepage. It does not establish customer demand, a live marketplace, legal clearance or a staffed advisory service.

## Recommended promise

**Good businesses deserve a next chapter.**

Make succession feel approachable while giving visitors a useful task they can complete now. The first product is a private preparation check and a downloadable next-step plan. Early access is an expression of interest, not a purchased service or an accepted application.

Recommended hero copy:

> **Good businesses deserve a next chapter.**
>
> A clearer starting point for the people building, buying and handing over small businesses. Explore what needs attention, then take your next steps with confidence.
>
> **Find your next step** · See how it works
>
> A free preparation check. No account or financial documents needed.

“With confidence” refers to a clearer starting point, not a successful acquisition. An even more literal alternative is “Leave with a practical plan for your next conversation.” Do not promise an exit, matching, financing, an increased valuation or professional acceptance.

Keep the inclusive hero; make the first path owner-first. Advisers remain the likely early commercial customer. Future operators should have a preparation path rather than a “browse deals” invitation when no listings exist.

## A concise homepage sequence

| Section | Headline and supporting copy | Working action |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | **Good businesses deserve a next chapter.** Use the supporting copy above. | Open the preparation check; scroll to process. |
| Audience choice | **Every next chapter starts somewhere.** Three clear paths below. | Each selection opens the check with the matching role already selected. |
| Product explanation | **Know what to bring into the conversation.** A useful handover starts with clear records, responsibilities and questions. | Show an unmistakably labeled sample plan; optionally open a complete sample. |
| Three steps | **From “where do I start?” to what comes next.** 1. Tell us where you are. 2. Find the gaps worth exploring. 3. Take your plan into the next conversation. | Open the check from the section's final CTA. |
| Principles | **A business is more than its balance sheet.** Make the people, customer relationships and everyday know-how visible alongside the records. | Three visual examples: records, responsibilities, continuity; these are topics rather than capabilities already delivered. |
| Early access | **Help shape a better handover.** We’re developing practical preparation tools for owners, future operators and the advisers who support them. | Prepare an early-access email; copyable fallback. |

Avoid an oversized navigation for a single homepage. Suggested links: How it works, Who it’s for, Questions, followed by one primary “Find your next step” button. Put early access in the body and footer. Every link must target real content or an actual interaction.

## Three audience cards

**For business owners**

### Make what you’ve built easier to pass on.

See which records, responsibilities and relationships need attention before your next succession conversation.

CTA: **Explore my next steps**

**For future operators**

### Prepare to take the reins.

Clarify the experience, support and questions you’ll need before taking on an existing business.

CTA: **Explore my next steps**

**For advisers**

### Bring more clarity to the first conversation.

Use a starting checklist to surface missing information and agree what needs attention next.

CTA: **Explore the checklist**

These are preparation outcomes the site can deliver now. Avoid “find your perfect business,” “meet qualified buyers,” “close faster,” or “save your clients hours” until those products and outcomes exist and are supported.

## The preparation check

Use “preparation check” or “starting-point check.” “Readiness assessment” is acceptable if the result clearly says it is self-reported. A precise readiness percentage, valuation, lender-ready badge or “approved” status would imply validation this questionnaire has not earned.

An effective small flow:

1. **Your perspective:** business owner; future operator; adviser. For advisers, make clear answers concern one hypothetical or authorized client situation; do not request client identity.
2. **Your horizon:** exploring; next few years; within a year; already in a transition. These labels organize a plan, not eligibility.
3. **What is clear already?** Select from plain, role-aware statements or answer “Yes / Partly / Not yet / Not sure” for five dimensions.
4. **Your next-step plan:** show the answers, two or three priorities, and a short checklist the visitor can save.

Recommended dimensions:

| Dimension | Owner/adviser wording | Future-operator wording |
|---|---|---|
| Records | Are the business’s recent financial records organized and understandable? | Do you know which financial records you would need to review? |
| Daily work | Could someone else see how the essential work gets done? | Do you understand the day-to-day work you would be taking on? |
| People | Is it clear which responsibilities need a successor or other support? | Have you identified the experience and support you would need? |
| Relationships | Are key customer, supplier and lease relationships documented? | Do you know which customer, supplier and lease relationships need investigation? |
| Next decision | Is there a clear next decision and someone qualified to help with it? | Is there a clear next decision and someone qualified to help with it? |

Use conditional priorities. Example: a records gap produces “Gather the records you already have and list what is missing. Agree the next document request with your accountant or adviser.” An operator-experience gap produces “Write down the essential operating responsibilities. Identify where you need training, an experienced partner or additional support.” Unknown answers should create questions to explore, not zeros that imply a bad business.

Do not recommend a transaction structure or select a successor based on these answers. Do not invent an exact timeframe to become sale-ready. If every answer is positive, say “You have a useful starting point for a more detailed conversation,” not “You’re ready to sell.”

### Result copy

**Your next chapter starts here.**

Here are the priorities suggested by your answers. Use them as a starting point for a conversation with the people helping you plan.

Actions: **Download my plan** · **Review my answers** · **Start again**

Brief qualification near the result: “Based on your answers. This is a preparation checklist, not a valuation or professional review.” The public homepage does not need an intrusive legal disclaimer in every section.

### Privacy and export wording

If answers are held only in browser memory and never sent to a server, say: **“Your answers stay in this browser. No account needed.”** If localStorage is used, instead say that answers are saved on this device and provide a working clear-data control. Avoid a broad “we collect no data” promise when hosting logs, analytics or third-party resources may still exist.

The downloaded plan should include role, horizon, answers, generated priorities, checklist, generation date and the self-reported scope note. Plain text or printable HTML is sufficient if the button does not call it a PDF. For a print-based PDF action, label it **“Print or save as PDF”**; do not claim a PDF download if it only opens a print dialog.

No email, personal name, company name, revenue, bank details or document upload is needed for this free action. The user should receive the result without joining a mailing list.

## Honest early-access interaction

Heading: **Help shape what comes next.**

Body: “Interested in tools for a real handover? Tell us which part you’re working on and what would make it easier.”

The action can collect role and an optional short note, then show a preview. Primary button: **Open email draft**. Supporting text: **“Opens your email app. Review and send it when you’re ready.”** A secondary **Copy message** action should work when no mail app is configured. A downloadable message is a useful additional fallback.

The destination must be an address the user has verified they control. Do not invent hello@maychant.com or silently use an unrelated inbox. Until a verified destination exists, a downloadable/copyable expression-of-interest draft is honest, but it is not a functioning request submission. Make that implementation dependency visible to the site owner before deployment.

Never show “You’re on the list,” “Application received,” or “We’ll be in touch” merely after clicking a mailto link. The site cannot verify that an email was sent. Appropriate status: **“Your draft is ready. Send it from your email app to share your interest.”** The draft should include only fields the visitor explicitly chooses to share; do not append their private preparation answers by default.

## Suggested FAQ copy

**What can I do here today?**
Complete a private preparation check and download a plan for your next conversation. You can also prepare an email expressing interest in early access.

**Can I buy or list a business here?**
Maychant is currently focused on preparation. There are no live business listings or buyer introductions on this site.

**Is the plan a valuation or professional review?**
No. It organizes priorities from your own answers. Your adviser, accountant, attorney or lender can help with the decisions that need their expertise.

**Do I need to upload financial documents?**
No. The preparation check uses a few broad questions and does not ask for financial documents.

**What happens to my answers?**
Use the answer that matches the implemented data flow: browser-memory only, or saved locally with a clear control. Do not describe planned privacy behavior as current fact.

**What does early access mean?**
We’re developing the next version. The email draft lets you describe your needs and express interest. Sending a message does not reserve a service or commit you to a purchase.

## Claims and visual traps to avoid

- No invented transaction volume, successful exits, users, reviews, partner logos, waitlist count or response-time promise.
- Photographs can illustrate real-world businesses without asserting those businesses are clients or for sale. Avoid a business-name/location/price overlay that makes a stock photo look like a live listing.
- Label a product illustration “Example plan” or “Illustration.” Do not animate fake matching notifications or claim a professional has approved a visitor's packet.
- “Private” must describe the actual answer handling. “Secure,” “encrypted” and professional-review claims need specific implementation evidence.
- Do not reuse macro market estimates as Maychant traction. The homepage does not need a national retirement statistic to be persuasive.
- Keep the shared purpose—continuity of good businesses—separate from proven company impact. “We’re building for better handovers” is supportable; “We preserve thousands of jobs” is not.
- Avoid “AI-powered” as the core promise when the live check is deterministic. If AI is not used, do not imply that it is.

## Functional QA gates for the implemented site

1. **Every visible CTA:** opens the intended modal or navigates to a real section; no inert buttons, fake account creation, placeholder social links or nonexistent legal pages.
2. **Three audience routes:** preselect the correct role and produce appropriately worded questions and priorities. Reopening from a different card must not retain an unnoticed incompatible role.
3. **Assessment completeness:** cannot advance without required selections; has a clear error; Back preserves valid answers; changing answers changes priorities; all-positive and all-unknown paths produce honest results.
4. **Download:** creates a real readable file containing the visitor's current answers and priorities. Confirm correct filename, format, date and escaping. A changed answer must be reflected in the next export.
5. **Privacy:** inspect network activity while changing answers and downloading. No answer payload, form submission, analytics event values or external AI request unless explicitly disclosed and intended. Verify reset actually clears stored answers.
6. **Early access:** email URI correctly encodes subject/body; role/note appear exactly once; a draft is not represented as sent; copy fallback works and reports clipboard failure honestly. Verify the recipient is supplied by the site owner.
7. **Keyboard and accessibility:** modal gets focus, traps it while open, closes with Escape and restores focus; controls have accessible labels; checkbox/radio semantics match behavior; error and result changes are announced; mobile menu is operable.
8. **Motion:** respect prefers-reduced-motion, retain useful content if animations fail, and avoid scroll locking or a hero effect that blocks the primary action. Decorative movement must not change reading order or interfere with input.
9. **Responsive behavior:** test a narrow phone, tablet and desktop. Check hero wrapping, sticky-nav anchors, modal scrolling, keyboard-visible form fields, FAQ focus, download and email actions.
10. **Claim review:** compare every headline, badge, number and state message with the capability actually present. The report's proposed professional acceptance metric is not a result this self-check can claim.

## Inspiration, translated to this product

The official reference homepages were reviewed for information architecture on 4 November 2026; this is not a visual screenshot audit.

- [Rocket Money](https://www.rocketmoney.com/): leads with a clear personal benefit, then explains concrete jobs. For Maychant, use the next conversation and preparation plan as the benefit; do not borrow the established company's social proof.
- [Acquire.com](https://acquire.com/): makes buyer and seller paths obvious. Maychant can use the audience clarity while directing each person to preparation, without implying available inventory or completed deals.
- [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/): organizes its main action around simple user choices. Maychant's audience/horizon selection can be equally approachable, without masquerading as a marketplace search.
- [Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/): has clear audience navigation and product-specific actions. Maychant should use that decisiveness with one real first action and visible limits on what is available today.

The original strategy remains a hypothesis: a narrowly scoped, professionally accepted preparation service may become a credible business. This homepage should test visitor understanding and interest while delivering a useful local tool. It should not pretend that the full service, buyer network or commercial validation already exists.
